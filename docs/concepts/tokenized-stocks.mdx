A tokenized stock is a token that tracks a real company's shares. It moves between wallets like any other token, settles in seconds, and trades around the clock on-chain.

On Base, tokenized stocks are issued by Coinbase as **B20 tokens**, Base's native token standard. Each is backed one-for-one by shares held with a regulated custodian, and priced by Chainlink tokenized-equity feeds. See [base.org/stocks](https://www.base.org/stocks).

## Dividends are reinvested, not paid out

Ordinary shares pay dividends in cash. B20 stocks don't send you anything. Instead, each token keeps a **multiplier**: the number of shares one token represents. When the company pays a cash dividend, it's converted into more shares and the multiplier goes up.

```text
Before a $0.91 dividend on a $530.73 share:   1 MSFTc = 1.0000 shares
After the dividend is converted to shares:    1 MSFTc = 1.0017 shares
```

Your token balance stays the same; what each token is worth goes up. NVIDIA's token already shows this: after its dividends since launch, `1 NVDAc = 1.0005 shares`. It's why the app shows the conversion next to every order.

## Splits use the same multiplier

A 2-for-1 stock split also doubles the multiplier, even though nobody received anything of value. Xero has to tell the two apart, so that a split is never paid out as if it were a huge dividend. It does this with a separate record of corporate actions, reported by independent keepers. See [Corporate actions](/developers/corporate-actions).
