---
title: "One share, two parts"
description: "How Xero splits a tokenized share into a share token and a dividend token."
---

Xero keeps one **vault** per stock and maturity date, for example "SCHD, October 15, 2027". When you deposit tokenized shares, the vault issues two tokens in equal amounts:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Share token" icon="certificate">
    Becomes the share on the maturity date. Redeem it then for the shares you deposited (or bought). Technical name: principal token, `PT`.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Dividend token" icon="coins">
    Collects every dividend paid until the maturity date, claimable at any time. Technical name: yield token, `YT`.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

One share token plus one dividend token is always worth exactly one share. Hold both and you can **rejoin** them into the share at any time before maturity. After maturity the share token redeems on its own.

## Units

Both tokens are counted in **units**, and one unit is one share at the moment you deposit. If you deposit 100 `SCHDc` while each holds 1.0345 shares, you get about 103.45 units of each token (minus the 0.05% split fee).

## What each token receives

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Share token">
    At maturity, each unit redeems for the tokenized shares worth one share. The dividends paid between deposit and maturity are not included; they went to the dividend token.

    After maturity, any further dividends stay with the share token, because it has become the share.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Dividend token">
    Every time a dividend is reinvested into the tokenized stock, the vault credits each dividend-token unit with its share of the new shares, in `SCHDc`. You can claim at any time; claims never expire.

    Xero keeps 3% of what dividend tokens collect as its protocol fee.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Why it's always fully backed

The vault never lends, stakes or moves the shares it holds. What it owes share-token holders plus what it owes dividend-token holders always equals what was deposited, at every block. This is checked in the contracts and tested against thousands of random sequences of deposits, dividends and splits.

## Stock splits

A split changes how many shares a token represents without anyone gaining value. Once a split is reported by Xero's keepers, the vault divides it out, so dividend-token holders are credited only real dividends and share-token holders keep exactly the same value. Until a split is reported, the vault pauses new deposits and dividend crediting rather than guess. Rejoining and claiming dividends already collected keep working throughout; redemption at maturity resumes once the split is reported.
