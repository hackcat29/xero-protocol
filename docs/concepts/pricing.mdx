---
title: "How prices are set"
description: "Where the discount and the upfront payout come from, and how to read them."
---

Each vault has a market where people trade its **dividend token** against the tokenized stock. That trading sets one number: the **expected dividend yield**, the yearly dividend rate the market is pricing in until maturity. Everything else follows from it.

## The dividend token's price

```text
dividend token price  ≈  share price × expected yield × years to maturity
```

For SCHD with a year to go and an expected yield of 3.55%:

```text
$28.40 × 3.55% × 1 year  ≈  $1.01
```

As maturity gets closer there are fewer dividends left to collect, so the dividend token's price drifts toward zero.

## The share token's price

Because the two parts add up to one share:

```text
share token price  =  share price − dividend token price
                   =  $28.40 − $1.01  =  $27.39
```

## The discount, and the upfront payout

These are the same number seen from two sides:

| | Formula | SCHD, 1 year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Discount** when you buy at a discount | dividend token price ÷ share price | 3.55% |
| **Upfront payout** when you sell your dividends | dividend token price ÷ share price | 3.55% of your position |

When you buy at a discount, the dividends you're skipping are being sold by someone else, who is paid exactly what you save.

## Return per year

Buying at a discount works like a zero-coupon bond measured in shares. If the share price stays flat, your return is:

```text
return per year  =  discount ÷ (1 − discount) ÷ years to maturity
                 =  3.55% ÷ 96.45% ÷ 1  ≈  3.68% a year
```

You also gain or lose with the share price, exactly as if you'd bought the share today.

## Why prices move

- **Trades**: buying dividend tokens raises the expected yield (and the discount); selling lowers it.
- **Time**: the dividend token's price decays as maturity approaches.
- **Share price**: both tokens' dollar prices move with the stock.
