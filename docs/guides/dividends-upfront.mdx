---
title: "Get dividends upfront"
description: "Turn the dividends your shares will pay into money today, and keep your shares."
---

If you own a dividend-paying stock, Xero can pay you its future dividends today. You choose a date, and you're paid now for every dividend the shares would pay until then. Your shares come back to you on that date.

## Example

You hold 100 Microsoft shares worth $53,073.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Paid to you today | about $377 (in `MSFTc`) |
| For the dividends until | Oct 15, 2027 |
| Your shares back on | Oct 15, 2027 |
| Upfront payout | 0.71% of your position |

In the meantime your 100 shares still rise and fall with the market. You keep all of that.

## How to do it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose a stock and a date">
    Open **Dividends upfront**. Each card shows what 100 shares would pay you today for the selected maturity.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter how many shares to use">
    The ticket shows the payout, the date your shares come back, and the payout as a share of your position.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Select **Review order**, check the payout and the date your shares come back, then **Confirm and get paid**. The payout arrives in your wallet in the same transaction.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Collect your shares at maturity">
    You hold a share token until the date. Redeem it from **Portfolio** for your shares.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Under the hood

The app calls `stripAndSellYield` on the Xero router: it splits your shares, sends you the share tokens, and sells the dividend tokens on the dividend market, paying the proceeds to you.

## Things to know

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The payout is in the tokenized stock">
    You're paid in the same tokenized stock, for example `MSFTc`, which you can hold or sell elsewhere. It's money now, not more stock locked up.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if dividends change?">
    If the company raises its dividend, the buyer of your dividends keeps the extra. If it cuts or suspends it, you've already been paid. Your share position is unaffected either way.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="It's not a loan">
    There's no interest, no repayment and no liquidation. You've sold something (future dividends) at a market price.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Taxes">
    Selling future dividends may be treated differently from receiving them. Ask a tax professional where you live.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
